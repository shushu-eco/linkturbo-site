Who is responsible

Link is made and offered by Shu, Spain. Questions about privacy: support@linkturbo.app.

The short version

Link works on your own computer. Your recordings, the moments Link finds in them and the clips it makes are never sent to us. We run no analytics, no advertising and no tracking in any Link product or on this website. There is no account to create. Link is free and open source: anyone can read the code and check what is said here (github.com/shushu-eco/link). Link Dev, the developer tools extension, reads only the sites you turn on, to show them to you, and sends nothing anywhere.

Link Lite Recorder (the browser extension)

What it does with data

  • It records the picture and sound of the Kick or Twitch tab you tell it to record, and saves that as a video file in your Downloads folder. The file stays on your computer.
  • While it records it counts how many chat messages arrive each second. Only the count is kept. It never keeps who wrote a message or what was written.
  • It reads the channel's name, the stream's title and category, and how many are watching, from the page you have open, and writes the channel, title and category into the recording so the streamer can be credited.
  • It notices when Twitch plays an ad, and notes when.

What it stores in the browser

  • Your settings (for example whether the rewind is on, and which skin the panel wears).
  • Where you last put its panel on the page.
  • A list of your last eight recordings (channel, length, its marks and their notes), so the panel can show them.
  • The day it was installed, and whether it has ever saved a recording (yes or no), for the page below that opens when you remove it.

This is kept in the browser's own extension storage on your computer and is removed when you remove the extension.

What it sends anywhere

Nothing. Your recordings, marks and chat counts are sent nowhere, and the extension makes no request of its own to any server. It has a button that opens Link's Discord invitation in a new tab if you press it.

It opens two pages of this website. When it is first installed it opens linkturbo.app/welcome, which shows how to make a first recording. When you remove it, your browser opens linkturbo.app/bye, which asks why. That page's address carries three things: the extension's version, how many days it was installed, and whether it ever saved a recording. They come after the # in the address, a part the browser does not send to any server. They are sent only if you choose a reason and press Send on that page (see "This website" below).

Versions before 0.5.0

Versions before 0.5.0 stopped after three recordings and asked for a licence key. They kept the number of recordings made (in the extension's storage, in the browser's synced storage, and as one small entry in the browser's storage for kick.com and twitch.tv), and, when a key was entered, sent that key to a licensing service, Lemon Squeezy (lemonsqueezy.com), to ask whether it was genuine. Version 0.5.0 does none of this, and removes the count and any key from the browser when it arrives. Your browser updates the extension by itself.

Link Dev (the developer tools extension)

Link Dev shows what a web page does (its network requests, its console and its storage), the way the browser's own developer tools do, in a panel you open with a button or Alt+Shift+D.

What it reads, and only on the sites you turn on

  • Local addresses (localhost and 127.0.0.1) are on from the start. Any other site is read only after you press Turn on for this site (or All sites) and the browser asks you to allow it. Settings > Sites turns a site off again.
  • On those sites it reads the requests the page makes (addresses, headers, what was sent and what came back), what the page writes to its console, and the page's cookies, local storage, session storage, IndexedDB and Cache storage, so it can show them to you.
  • Secrets (tokens, cookies, passwords and fields whose name says secret) are hidden on screen and in every file you export, until you press the eye.

What it stores in the browser

  • Your settings and the rules you make (Mock, Block, Delay, Redirect, Headers), in the browser's extension storage.
  • The lists it records for each tab, in the browser's session storage, which is emptied when the browser closes.

This stays on your computer and is removed when you remove the extension.

What it sends anywhere

Nothing. Link Dev makes no request of its own to any server, and has no account, analytics or tracking. It sends a request only when you ask it to: Edit and resend sends the request you edited from the page itself, to the address you gave. Files you export (a HAR file, a report, a summary) are saved where you choose.

Your own scripts

A Mock rule can answer with a small script you write. It runs in a sandboxed page inside the extension, never in the web page, and is kept with your settings.

Link TURBO (the Windows program)

It is downloaded as an installer or a zip, and its code is public. As built today:

  • Recording, finding moments, speech-to-text, editing and rendering all happen on your computer.
  • If you choose to connect a service (Kick, YouTube, Instagram, Discord, Spotify), Link talks to that service directly from your computer, using keys or sign-ins that are your own. Those are stored on your computer, in your user folder, and are not sent to us. Each of those services has its own privacy policy, which applies to what you do there.
  • When you post a clip, the clip and its title and text are sent to the service you post to. Nothing else is.
  • The program can check whether a newer version exists and download it.
  • Its built-in browser panel can block ads with public filter lists. Those lists are downloaded from their publishers when the program runs.

The Discord server

Link's community server runs on Discord. What you write and do there is handled by Discord under Discord's own privacy policy. We see what any server owner sees: your Discord name, your messages in the server, and the roles you chose when you joined.

The code on GitHub

Link's code is kept on GitHub. If you open an issue, comment or send a change there, GitHub handles it under GitHub's own privacy policy, and what you write is public.

This website

linkturbo.app is a plain page. It sets no cookies and loads nothing from other companies. The company that hosts it keeps ordinary server logs (such as IP addresses) for a short time, as every web host does.

The page linkturbo.app/bye, which opens when Link Lite Recorder is removed, asks why. If you do not press Send, nothing is sent. If you answer and press Send, the answer goes to Form.taxi, a form service, which e-mails it to support@linkturbo.app. What it holds: the reason you chose, what you wrote, the extension's version, how many days it was installed and whether it saved a recording. As with any website, Form.taxi also receives your IP address and the name of your browser, and shows us a rough location (such as the country) worked out from the IP address. We use the answers only to decide what to fix. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in learning why people remove Link Lite Recorder, so we can make it better (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); answering is up to you.

Form.taxi is run by wrkt*biz Reinhard Söllradl in Austria and processes the answers for us under a data processing agreement (Art. 28 GDPR). Its servers are in Germany and it sends its e-mails through Mailjet, in the EU; nothing goes outside the EU. It keeps an answer for at most one year. We delete the answers there and in our inbox once they have been read and counted. Its privacy policy: form.taxi/en/privacy.

Children

Link is not directed at children. The Discord server requires the minimum age Discord sets for your country (13 or older).

Your rights

Because Link does not collect your data, there is normally nothing for us to give you, correct or delete. If you have written to us by email, you can ask us to delete that correspondence. If you live in the EU or the UK you also have the right to complain to your data protection authority; in Spain that is the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (aepd.es).

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted where this one is, with a new date, and announced in the Discord server's announcements channel.
